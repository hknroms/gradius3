Cube Rush Analysis

Results of investigating how the cube heights are decided in the stage 9 Cube Rush of Gradius III (arcade, version 313), based on the program and its behavior on hardware-equivalent emulation (MAME). The mod ROM's CUBE HEIGHT / SHIFT / TYPE and CUBE INFO are built on these results.

How the Height Is Decided

At the moment a cube appears, the game looks at a timer that increases by 1 on every screen refresh (16 bits) and decides the height from its value.

  • Height number = bits 3–6 (counting from the bottom) of (low 8 bits of the timer xor high 8 bits). That gives 16 values, 0–15.
  • The height for each number comes from a table, and four pairs near the middle (5 and 6, 7 and 8, 9 and 10, 11 and 12) share the same height, so there are 12 actual heights.
  • Everything other than the height is decided after the cube appears: the direction it flies in depends on the ship's position at that time, and the speed depends on the rank.

Appearance Timing

  • The timing of the 2nd cube onward is fixed as a number of frames after the 1st, and is the same every time.
  • Every interval is a multiple of 16 frames (16–80 frames). The 99th cube appears 4208 frames after the 1st.
  • These intervals are counted in frames where the game logic advanced, while the timer increases on every screen refresh (this leads to the "drift" described below).

How the Sequence Is Decided

Because the intervals are fixed, the timer value at the moment the 1st cube appears alone decides the heights of all 99 cubes (when there is no slowdown). There are 2880 different sequences in all.

What H, S and T Mean, and How to Choose Them

CUBE HEIGHT (H), SHIFT (S) and TYPE (T) in the mod ROM's menu are numbers given to these 2880 sequences (16 × 4 × 45 = 2880).

  • Only H has a meaning you can see: it is the height of the 1st cube.
  • S and T are not values that exist in the game. They are "catalog numbers" for telling the sequences apart. You don't work out a sequence from them.
  • So the way to use them is: look at the sequences first, note the H, S and T of one you like, and pick them in the menu. In "View a Sequence" below, choosing H, S and T shows all 99 cubes as a chart and as numbers.

Each of H, S and T in the menu can also be set to RANDOM (as in the original game).

Finding the Number of the Sequence You Are Playing

  • Even when playing with RANDOM, turning CUBE INFO ON in the pause menu shows the H, S and T of the current rush once the 1st cube appears.
  • When a sequence you want to practice comes up, note its H, S and T. Next time, set them in the menu to get the same sequence.
  • If slowdown changes the sequence, CUBE INFO shows "LAG:…" (see "Drift Caused by Slowdown" below).

Reproducing a Sequence You Remember from the Real Machine

  • Sequences on the real (original) machine are also among these 2880. Once you know the number, the mod ROM can reproduce it exactly.
  • Use "Find a Sequence You Remember" below: enter the heights you remember, starting from the 1st cube.
  • How much you need to remember:
    • If you know the exact heights of the first 25 cubes, at most 12 candidates remain.
    • It narrows down to one when you know about the first 50.
    • With vague memory ("somewhere high", "somewhere low"), more candidates remain. Compare the charts of the remaining candidates and pick the one that looks right.
  • If the real machine had slowdown, the sequence is shifted from that point on. If what you remember stops matching every candidate partway, enter only the part that matches.
Details (how the numbers are assigned)
  • The timer has 65536 possible values, but the lowest 2 bits are not used in the height calculation, so there are 2880 actually different sequences.
  • H: the height number of the 1st cube.
  • S: bits 3–4 (from the bottom) of the timer at the moment of the 1st cube. Since the intervals are multiples of 16, these 2 bits are the same for all 99 cubes.
  • T: the remaining difference within the same H and S. There are 45, numbered in increasing order of the timer value. Where an upper digit of the timer carries over is where the T "boundaries" are.
  • Even counting height numbers that share a height as one, all 2880 sequences are different.
  • We chose H5, S B, T12 in MAME, recorded a full run, and confirmed that all 99 heights match the calculation.

Drift Caused by Slowdown (LAG)

  • The timer still increases on slowdown frames, but the appearance interval does not advance. So for every frame of slowdown, the timer gets one ahead of the calculation.
  • The drift never goes back; it keeps accumulating for the rest of that rush, with no upper limit.
  • Since the lowest 2 bits are not used in the height calculation, depending on those bits at the moment of the 1st cube, a drift of 1–3 frames may not change the sequence. With 4 or more frames of drift, the heights from that point on are almost completely different.
  • The mod ROM's CUBE INFO shows "LAG:12+0005" (number of the first cube whose sequence changed + total frames of drift) only when the sequence has actually changed.
  • In a test running all 99 cubes while invincible with a large pile of stuck cubes, there were 0 frames of slowdown. How much drift occurs while firing continuously with Options and Laser has not been measured yet.

Sticking Check

  • A cube "sticks" and becomes part of the background when it hits a wall or another cube, or reaches the left edge or the top/bottom edges of the screen. This check is done by the sub CPU.
  • Cubes that leave the screen do not stick. In testing, 97 of 99 cubes stuck.
  • In the mod ROM's Cube Rush, each cube that sticks gives 1000 points. This does not affect the high score, rankings or extra lives.

Where Cubes React (decided by the cube number)

A cube appears at the right edge (around x=350), drifts straight left for a fixed time (2 dots per frame), and the moment that time ends it aims once at the ship's position at that moment and flies straight at it. It does not re-aim.

  • The wait time comes from a 16-entry table indexed by the cube number divided by 2. Cubes move the same way in pairs, and the pattern repeats every 32 cubes (only the 1st cube is on its own). It is unrelated to the height sequence (H, S, T).
  • The reacting position is "350 − (wait time × 2)" dots. Cubes with a wait time of 176 frames or more reach the left edge before reacting and simply stick there (they do not react).
  • The flying speed depends on the rank (ranks 0–5: 4 dots per frame; then 0.5 dots faster for every 2 ranks; 6.5 dots at ranks 14–15, and 7 dots at 16 and above).
  • Whether a cube "reacts in front" or "comes from behind" depends on the ship's position. The table below assumes the ship stays at its initial position (x=111).
